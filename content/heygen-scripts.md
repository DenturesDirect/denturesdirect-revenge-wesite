## 2 — Implant Dentures vs. Traditional Dentures
`slug: implant-dentures-vs-traditional-dentures`

> **On-screen title:** "Implant vs. traditional dentures — the honest comparison"

Not everyone needs implants — but everyone deserves the honest trade-offs before deciding.
Here's the real difference. Stability: a traditional lower rests on your gums, which is why
it floats and clicks. An implant denture snaps onto three or four implants and simply
doesn't move — within a week, patients tell me they forget they're wearing it. Chewing:
traditional dentures give you maybe twenty to thirty percent of natural bite force; implants
give you far more, because the force goes into your jaw instead of pinching your gums. And
here's what most people don't know — a traditional denture actually speeds up bone loss,
while implants help slow it. If your budget's tight and your jaw is stable, a good digital
denture serves you well. If you're tired of adhesive, implants solve the physics. Call me

snap-on overdenture clips onto three implants on the bottom or four on top, and you take it

## 6 — Acrylic vs. Cast vs. Flexible Partial Dentures
`slug: acrylic-vs-cast-vs-flexible-partial-dentures`

> **On-screen title:** "Three kinds of partial denture — which one?"

Missing some, but not all, of your teeth? A partial fills the gaps, and there are three main
types. Acrylic is the budget-friendly one — teeth set in a pink base, easy to adjust and add
to later, though a little bulkier. Cast metal uses a slim cobalt-chrome framework — it's the
gold standard for durability and fit, thin and strong, the best long-term investment for most
people, with the only trade-off being that a clasp might show. And flexible is a thin,
gum-coloured nylon with no metal clasps at all — nearly invisible and very comfortable,
though harder to add to down the road. We make all three digitally in our own lab, and here's
my promise: I'll recommend the one that fits your mouth and your goals — not the most

## 7 — Denture Relines: Hard vs. Soft
`slug: denture-relines-hard-vs-soft`

> **On-screen title:** "Loose denture? You might just need a reline."

If your denture used to fit and now it slips, clicks, or rubs sore spots — you probably don't
need a whole new denture. You might just need a reline. Here's why it happens: your gums and
jaw slowly change shape over the years, but the denture stays the same, so a gap opens up —
and that gap is what causes the movement and the sore spots. A reline refits the inside of
your existing denture to your gums as they are today. A hard reline adds durable acrylic and
lasts for years. A soft reline uses a cushioned material — gentle on tender or bony ridges,
and great right after extractions. Now, if your denture's cracked or the teeth are worn flat,
a reline is just a band-aid — and if you're relining that lower over and over, that's the
classic sign it's time to think about implants. Call us: four-one-six, two-four-five,
seven-four-seven-four.
